Shri Nanda Devi Raj Jat Yatra

"The Poignant Himalayan Farewell: Accompanying Goddess Nanda to Mount Trishul"

In Uttarakhand folk memory, Goddess Nanda is not merely a distant deity, but the beloved elder daughter (Dhyani) of every mountain household. Once every 12 years, thousands of devotees escort her on an arduous 22-day barefoot pilgrimage across river valleys, high alpine meadows (Bedni Bugyal), and glaciated moraines, walking from her maternal home in Nauti to her divine consort Lord Shiva's abode at Mount Trishul.

Maternal Lore

The Daughter's Farewell (Vidaai)

Unlike typical temple pilgrimages, the Raj Jat is an emotional family journey. When Goddess Nanda leaves Nauti village, women across Chamoli sing tearful Mangal Geet as parents bid goodbye to a married daughter returning to her austere in-laws in the freezing snows of Mount Trishul.

Centuries-old oral folklore tradition
Divine Marvel

The Four-Horned Ram & Chhantoli

Before each Yatra, a male sheep with four horns (Chausingha Khadu) is miraculously born in Kasuwa village. Adorned with vermilion and silk, the sacred ram leads the entire 280-km procession without any rope or guidance, accompanied by the royal golden ringed umbrella (Chhantoli) carried by Garhwal's Parmar royalty.

Walks freely at head of procession
Cultural Unification

Garhwal & Kumaon Sacred Union

At the holy confluence of Nandkeshari on the Pranmati River, the grand Kumaoni procession arriving from Almora, Nainital, and Johar Valley unites with the Garhwal devotees. Both regions dissolve all political and historical divides, walking as one family behind the Goddess.

Confluence Camp at Nandkeshari (Day 6)

The Last Location & Sacred Culmination

Homkund (4,061 m) — Where the Yatra Ends & Eternity Begins

Situated in an immense glaciated moraine amphitheater right beneath the precipitous southern rock-and-ice face of Mount Trishul (7,120 m) and Mount Nanda Ghunti (6,309 m), Homkund is the final terrestrial boundary permitted to mortals.

What Happens at the Last Location:

At Homkund, the hereditary Nautiyal priests perform the concluding Maha-Hawan. In an intensely emotional ceremony, pilgrims bid their final tearful farewell to Goddess Nanda. The four-horned ram (Chausingha Khadu) is adorned with red vermilion, silken vestments, and offerings, and released into the snowfields.

The Miracle of Departure:

Miraculously, the ram sheds tears, turns toward the soaring peaks of Mount Trishul, and walks ahead alone into the glaciated wilderness without ever looking back, carrying the prayers of Uttarakhand to Lord Shiva. No human is permitted to walk past Homkund. Pilgrims turn back via Sutol and Ghat.

Extreme glaciated altitude (oxygen < 60%, sub-zero frost). Strict leave-no-trace ecological rules apply.
